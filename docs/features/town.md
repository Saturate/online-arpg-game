# Town, the world and dungeons

Status: Live. Wilds maps, the town editor and dungeons since 2026-09-28; public worlds, the walk-in town, zones and waypoints since 2026-09-28 and 2026-09-29; gates on the map edge since 2026-09-29. The editor's palette (every prop and decor asset, camp fires, solid decor) built 2026-09-30, not pushed. The six zones became one seamless world round the town on 2026-10-01, not pushed ([world-map.md](world-map.md)).

## What it does

- **Everyone enters the world in the town, Emberwatch,** a shared safe area with no damage and no monsters, in the middle of the world. Its three fenced gates (north, east, south) are where the world's three roads start, so leaving town is a walk with no load.
- **Town stations:** the stash chest, the trader stall and the forge (the weapon rack) nearest the spawn, a waypoint, and the Arena entrance, a sunken stone drum with an archway ([stash.md](stash.md), [items.md](items.md), [forge.md](forge.md), [arena.md](arena.md)). Stations open on a click after the hero walks into reach, and close when you walk away.
- **One seamless world** round the town, its regions themed after the six zones there were before (Mossy Barrens round the town; Gloomvale and The Hollows up the north road, Ashen Steppe and Sunscorched Dunes along the east road, Thornwood down the south road), with monster levels rising from 1 at the gates to 25 at each road's far end. Everything about it is in [world-map.md](world-map.md).
- **Waypoints:** touching one activates it for that character; click it to open the menu and travel to any waypoint you have found, within the world. Everyone has the town's.
- **Dungeons:** the world has 11 entrances, at dead ends (two per region, one in the home region). An entrance leads to an antechamber, a small safe hall with a ready check (R); when everyone inside is ready, a 3 s countdown sends them into a fresh run together. Killing the boss clears the run, opens a cache ([loot.md](loot.md)) and opens the exit; until then the only way out is the portal back to the antechamber at the entrance. Both the exit and the antechamber's way out lead back to the world beside the entrance.
- **Worlds:** everyone lands in a shared public copy of the world, up to 8 players; a party can open its own party world ([chat-and-parties.md](chat-and-parties.md)).
- **The town editor (F2 in town, builders and up)** edits the town layout live, placing anything from a palette of 244 pieces: the layout's 13 props, 229 KayKit models and two fires built in code (camp fire, brazier).

## Why

### Worlds and instances

- **No global town, but no private games by default either.** Everyone lands in a public copy of the world on the owner's world seed (admin Settings), up to 8 players (`INSTANCE_CAPACITY`); when all are full a new copy opens. Players never pick seeds. A party world gets a seed the server picks from a counter, and only the party can enter it.
- **Rooms:** the server's `RoomManager` hosts every room inside a world instance: its one world room (the town and every region), dungeon and Arena antechambers and their runs, and builders' sandboxes. Portals move a character (class, name, items, equipment, stance) between rooms in memory, saving on every move; waypoints and party teleports inside the world are a move within the room.
- **The world room is created when someone first enters the copy** and closed after 5 minutes empty. An instance goes once it has no rooms and no members. Its seed comes from the instance seed, so it regenerates identically with fresh monsters, like re-entering a D2 area.
- **Maps travel as descriptors, not geometry.** The welcome message carries `{ kind: 'world', seed, layout }`. The client regenerates the identical map with the shared generator, so collision prediction stays exact.
- **Join and reconnect** land in the town of your party world if it has room, otherwise the public world.

### The town inside the world

- **The town layout is built at its own origin and moved to the middle of the world** (`placeTown`), where it is marked as a safe zone; the map records where (`WorldMap.townAt`), so the town editor keeps working in town coordinates.
- **The safe zone rule lives in `isTargetable`,** the one check both monster aggro and damage go through. Monsters lose their target at the gate and leash home. Packs and dungeon entrances keep outside aggro range of the town.
- **The Arena entrance sits off the roads,** so walking out of town never drops you into it.
- **The world never pauses.** Esc pauses a room only when you are alone in it, it is not shared and it is not an Arena run (`Room.canPause`). The town is where everyone arrives, so the world room counts as shared even when one player is in it; antechambers and dungeons pause when you are alone.

### Waypoints

- **The server re-checks** that you are standing on a waypoint (its radius plus 120) and that you have activated the destination. Saves from before waypoints existed are given the town's on load.
- **The menu opens when the waypoint is clicked** and closes when you walk off.
- **Waypoints** are drawn as a raised stone slab with four rune stones and a dim rune circle, not as a portal.
- **Arriving never triggers the way back:** you land the waypoint's radius plus 70 units off it, toward the middle of the map. Zone gates went with the zones; the roads are the way between regions.

### World content

- **The world** ([world-map.md](world-map.md)): roads branching from the town, rivers bridged where roads cross them, ridges between the roads' sectors and loose ones in the regions, forests, ruins (plazas with pillar rings), loose rocks. A standalone Wilds (tests and tools) keeps the zone layout: 1 or 2 rivers with 3 bridges each, a road east from its camp. Both are a plan plus content generated per chunk (forests, loose rocks, bones, packs); see [world-streaming.md](world-streaming.md#step-3-generation-by-chunk).
- **Packs** are placed only on cells reachable from the spawn (past the plan's rivers, ridges, ruins and town) and never within 750 units of it, or within aggro range of the town. Monster level rises with distance (in the world, along the roads), and a boss pack stands over an ordinary pack with an escort: in a Wilds at the farthest reachable spot of a few hundred tries, in the world at each region's deepest dead end. A chunk's packs spawn when it first wakes. The standard 5600x4200 Wilds map has 30 packs; the world scales that by area (about 216, more further out, plus rares at dead ends).
- **Monsters come from the biome's pool** by level ([monsters.md](monsters.md)).
- **Dungeon entrances:** in the world at the dead ends the plan gives them; in a Wilds 2 on reachable ground at least 1400 units from the spawn, away from packs and other portals. Monster level is the ground's around the entrance, plus 1. The dungeon seed comes from the map seed, so everyone in one instance shares the same antechamber.
- **Camps** (built 2026-09-30, not pushed; `world/camps.ts`): small camps out in the world's side valleys, up to 5 in the home region and 4 in each other, at the spots the world plan picks. Each is a camp fire on a circle of trampled dirt with 3 to 6 supplies around it (crates, barrels, kegs, sacks, a bucket), usually a log to sit on, and a tent facing the fire (56% of camps; the tent is solid) or a cart (26%). About a quarter are guarded: a pack rolled from the zone's pool at the local level stands on the fire. The fire is the `campfire` decor, so it burns, smokes and lights the ground at night like the forge's.
  - **Placement rules:** the whole 120 unit circle must be open ground (no rocks, trees, walls, water or bridges), reachable from the spawn, at least 750 from it, out of the town's aggro pad, 450 beyond any portal, waypoint or gate trigger, clear of every road, gate road and plaza (ruins, dungeon entrances) by 30 units, and 1100 from the next camp. A guarded camp needs the usual pack spacing (420) from other packs; an unguarded one keeps 380 from packs so it does not read as guarded.
  - **Part of the plan** (since step 3): camps are placed after the waypoints, bosses and entrances and before any chunk content, on their own random stream (`Rng.stream(seed, 'camps')`). Whether a camp is guarded no longer depends on packs near it (they do not exist yet); instead chunk packs keep 420 from a guarded camp and 380 from an unguarded one, and trees and rocks keep off every camp.
  - **Cost** (measured in the home zone before the world, and before step 2 put batches per chunk): camp props go into the `PropBatch` like all decor, so each new kind of prop (tent, keg, open crate and so on; 6 in the home zone for seed 904226) is one instanced draw. Each camp fire adds 2 draws (stones and logs) and its dirt circle 1, and those are culled off screen. A camp at night measured 124 draw calls in the home zone.

### Dungeons

- **The antechamber** is safe. A ready check (R or the panel button) starts a 3 s countdown once everyone inside is ready; anyone arriving or un-readying cancels it. While a run is live the gate lets latecomers straight in. The antechamber only closes once no run is live.
- **Killing the boss marks the run cleared once:** it opens the cache ([loot.md](loot.md)), everyone inside gets a banner and the chat line "The way out opens in the boss chamber", and the antechamber shows "Last run cleared". Dev tools' Kill all counts as killing the boss.
- **The way back is open from the start:** a portal beside the entrance spawn, "Back to the antechamber", takes you to the run's antechamber, where the gate lets you back into the same live run. It replaced the old "Leave" portal, which went straight to the Wilds.
- **The exit is sealed until the boss dies.** It stands at its usual spot in the boss room, east of the boss pack's centre. Before the boss dies nothing draws, labels or maps it, and standing on it does nothing. When the boss dies it rises out of the floor over 1.2 s with a violet shockwave, a flash of light and motes (the VFX system, `mixed` style), then shows on the minimap (once explored) and gets its "Exit" label. Anyone arriving later (the gate, a party teleport) sees it already standing, without the burst. It leads back to the world, beside the entrance the run was entered from.
- **Nobody is yanked out while looting:** a player standing on the exit when it opens gets 3 s before it takes them, and loot lying on or beside it (the boss's drops, the cache, gold) moves 144 units toward the boss room's centre.
- **Every generated dungeon has a boss,** so every dungeon now has a sealed exit. A map without a boss pack would have no sealed portal; nothing like that exists today.
- **Each run is its own room** with a fresh layout, seeded from the dungeon seed, the run number and the level.
- **Generation:** rooms sit in a 4 by 3 slot grid, joined by a randomised DFS spanning tree plus 3 extra corridors, so there are loops without a maze. Carving uses the 40-unit nav cell, so walls line up exactly with pathfinding. Only rock cells touching floor become wall boxes, merged into rectangles, which keeps collision in the hundreds of shapes. Packs get harder with graph depth (up to +2 levels), and the boss room is the deepest room and holds the (sealed) exit. Dungeons are crypts (even seeds) or caves (odd).
- **Rendering:** floors and walls are each one merged mesh with world-space UVs. Walls are drawn 44 units tall rather than 70, so a south wall does not hide the hero. Torches are lit by the shared light budget (see Lighting below); underground it is always night for them.

### Collision and pathing

- **Collision is static per map:** circles, capsules and rotated boxes in a spatial hash. Water blocks walking but not shots.
- **Movement is sub-stepped** (at most 20 units per step), so dashes cannot tunnel. When a step makes less than 30% of its intended progress, the mover tries the direction turned 45 degrees each way, which stops head-on sticking against round obstacles.
- **Players pass through monsters and each other;** only map obstacles block them. This keeps prediction exact, because the predicted step depends only on input and the static map.
- **Roads give +10% movement speed** to players, minions and monsters, computed in shared movement so prediction stays exact.
- **Map edges:** grass runs 1500 units past the edge under an instanced forest with rocks and mountain rings, so the camera never sees void.
- **Fences are drawn on the line that blocks walking** (fixed 2026-10-01; the backlog's "hero stuck on a fence corner leaving town"). A fence prop blocks as a capsule of radius 8 along its line, drawn as `fence_wood_straight` tiles every 44 units. The KayKit fence is modelled on the edge of a hex tile, 47 units (at scale 1) from the model's origin, and segments were placed by their origin, so every town fence was drawn about 40 units to one side of its collision: inward on the north and east fences, outward on the south and west ones and on builders' fences by their angle. On the south and west the hero stopped in open ground short of the drawn fence, and at a gate it caught on the end of a fence it could not see, beside the opening it could. Collision, nav cells and the gaps were never wrong: the gates are 180 wide (164 between the capsules, a hero is 28), no solid decor stands near them, and walking out of every gate from the spawn and from beside each gate post (default and live town) never stalls. Segments (`length` fits, fences and walls) are now centred on their line by the model's bounds (`placementMatrix` in `render/propBatch.ts`); other props stay placed by their origin, which the decor footprints' `ox`/`oz` assume. The dungeon wall used for town walls was already centred.
- **Thin solid decor blocks at least 16 units thick** (`decorCollision`, the fence prop's 8 radius as a half thickness). Movement steps up to 20 units, so a dash went straight through a solid graveyard split fence (2 units thick) or wood fence piece (5); walking (10 to 12 units a tick) did not. No live town has solid decor yet, so nothing changed for players.
- **Minions and monsters see the same fences:** the nav grid blocks a cell whose centre is within the agent radius (14) of an obstacle, the same test the hero's collision makes, so every fence blocks a full row of cells along its length and each gate's middle cell is open (`test/townGates.test.ts`, which also checks a flow field from outside each gate reaches the spawn).

### Lighting

- **Every flame and lamp casts light at night:** lamp posts, dungeon torches, candles, standing and post lanterns and shrine candles from decor, the forge fire, the camp fire, the Arena building's torches, portals (in their own colour) and waypoints (a low cold light). Each gets a pool of light on the ground with a slow two-sine flicker (never a strobe). They fade in at dusk and out at dawn with the night factor; by day outdoors they cast nothing, so the day look is unchanged. Underground they are always lit. The flames themselves (noise-shader flames, embers, smoke, coals) are drawn by the world fires in [vfx.md](vfx.md#world-fires) and pulse with these lights.
- **The light budget** (`apps/client/src/render/lights.ts`) is shared by world lights and spells. A fixed pool of 8 real point lights goes to the sources that score highest (priority first, then brightness and distance to the camera focus); every other visible source is drawn as a ground pool, one instanced draw call for all of them. A source that wins or loses its real light fades over 0.45 s, and its ground pool fades the other way, so nothing pops. The pool size never changes because the light count is compiled into every material's shader; switching lights on and off as you walk would recompile shaders mid-fight. No shadows: they would cost a shadow map per light.
- **Why pools of light and not flat discs:** real lights fall off with height^1.2 scaled into the intensity, so a source's `intensity` is the light straight below it and it thins out over its radius. The old per-lamp lights used physical decay at these distances (hundreds of units), which left a lamp contributing about 0.1% of its intensity at its own base: the lamps outdoors lit nothing.
- **Crowding:** when many lights pile up where the hero stands (a volley of spells, a ring of torches), every light is scaled down together so the total near the hero stays at about 4 (a single torch is 3). The ground pools use screen blending, so overlapping pools saturate softly instead of burning white. In a test with 64 spell lights around the hero the scale settles near 0.2. Spell ground pools also divide by the square root of how many spell pools overlap each one (world lights keep theirs), because a dozen stacked zone and bolt pools washed the ground a flat pastel.
- **Reach:** torches 400, lanterns 340 (they were 480 and 420; a street of lanterns lit the whole square).
- **Day and night per map** (`nightModeOf` in `daylight.ts`, read by the scene and by `Effects`, so the game, the Spell Studio and the VFX bench agree): town and wilds follow the clock, the dungeon, staging room and Arena pit are always night, and the flat test maps (Sandbox, Testground, Studio) have no night at all.
- **The hero's light** at night is a warm pool around them (decay 1.4, 120 units up, 2.2 at their feet times `heroLight`, default 0.8), reaching `heroLightRadius` (default 520; it was 1 over 700, which lit most of the screen). Party members carry a faint one (0.55 of the hero's setting, priority 1). By day the hero's light is as before; underground only the admin multiplier applies.
- **Enemies at night:** a cold moonlight rim on enemies only (`nightRim.ts`), `0.34 * nightFactor()` on the procedural rigs and 0.8 of that on KayKit monsters (their materials are cached apart for the enemy copy of a def, so a minion on the same model stays unrimmed). Heroes and minions get no rim. Procedural enemy rig parts darker than sRGB 0x30 (non-glowing) are lifted to it, keeping their hue; hero placeholders and minion rigs keep their own colours. Before this, 23 of 53 procedural monster types showed only their eyes at night.
- **Keeping the mood:** the global night brightness is untouched, so the ground and sky away from lights stay as dark as before. Measured on the town square at `?time=0.9` (mean 0 to 255 luminance): the 300 px round the hero went from 9.5 to 60, the screen edges from 1.5 to 8; day (`?time=0.3`) differs by 0.15 on average (the flames' flicker).
- **Admin settings:** `heroLight`, `heroLightRadius` and `lampLight` ([accounts-admin.md](accounts-admin.md)), sent to clients with the rest of the lighting.

Light API, for torches and spells alike. Allocation-free per frame; emits are read by the next frame's budget update (in `WorldScene.follow`) and then cleared, so a source that stops emitting fades out:

```ts
import { emitLight, entityLightKey, lightKey } from '../render/lights.js';

const key = lightKey(); // once per source (a projectile, a zone); never reused
// or entityLightKey(entityId, channel 0..7): the same key from any client, no table needed

// Every frame the source is alive: ground x/y, height above ground, 0xRRGGBB, intensity (the light
// straight below; a torch is 3), radius (world units), priority (0 world, 1 party, higher for big
// spells), day (0 night only, 1 as bright by day; spells mostly 0.2 to 0.4).
emitLight(key, x, y, height, color, intensity, radius, priority, day);
```

World lights are `StaticLight` records that `buildWorld` returns in `BuiltWorld.lights`; `sceneLights.stats` has `{ real, pools, sources, crowd }` for the last frame.

Performance (headless Chrome on the dev Mac, 1280x720, GPU time from `EXT_disjoint_timer_query`, 5 s samples, noisy to about 1 ms): town square at night 9.45 ms mean before and 8.63 ms after, 275 draw calls both; a dungeon corridor 3.65 ms before and 4.32 ms after (54 to 55 draw calls: 6 real torch lights became 8); the town with 64 extra spell lights 6.29 ms and 276 draw calls. CPU per frame stays 1 to 2 ms.

### The town editor

- **F2 in town opens it** for roles with the `townEdit` permission (builders, admins, the owner); the admin page points to it.
- **Tools:** select and move, place prop, path brush, plaza brush, erase (keys 1 to 5). Q/E rotate, [ and ] scale (or line length, plaza radius or brush width), G snaps to 20 units, Ctrl+Z undoes (80 steps), Delete removes, WASD pans and the wheel zooms.
- **Save sends the layout to the server,** which validates every field, writes `TOWN_LAYOUT` (default `apps/server/data/town-layout.json`, `/data/town-layout.json` in production) atomically, and rebuilds the world room in every world instance with everyone in it carried over to where they stood, and the loot on the ground and the opened chests with them. The world's roads start at the town's gates, so a save that moves a gate gets a new world round it ([world-map.md](world-map.md)).
- **Portals and the spawn point can be moved but not deleted** in the editor, and a layout without a Wilds portal is rejected by the server.
- **Decor can be placed from the palette, selected, moved, rotated, scaled and deleted,** so the forge (the weapon rack nearest the spawn) moves like any prop and a second rack placed nearer the spawn becomes the forge.
- **Click cycling:** clicking again at the same spot (within 6 px) over the same objects selects the next one underneath, front to back, and wraps; the panel and a small tag by the cursor show "2 of 4". A click elsewhere starts over, except that a click over the object already selected keeps it, so something picked in the layer list can be dragged out of a crowd. Alt+click lists everything under the cursor to pick from (Esc closes it). Front to back means stations, portals and the spawn first, then props and decor smallest first, then paths, then plazas. A press only becomes a drag after the cursor moves 4 px, so cycling clicks never nudge an object onto the grid or cost an undo step.
- **Layer list** in the editor panel: every town object grouped as Stations (forge, stash, trader, portals and the Arena entrance, the spawn point with the waypoint beside it), Buildings, Props, Lights and decor, Nature and Ground (paths, plazas), with a search box (every word must match the name or group). Hovering a row outlines the object in the world, a click selects it (locked ones too), a double click centres the camera on it.
- **Hide and lock** per row are editor-only: hidden objects are left out of the preview and cannot be clicked, locked ones let clicks pass through to what is under them. Neither is saved in the layout or sent to anyone; they follow their objects through deletes and undo, survive the editor reopening after a save, and are cleared when the town changed in between.
- **Stations are easier to hit:** the forge, stash and trader take clicks 26 units beyond their footprint (other objects 6), and hovering in the select tool outlines what a click would pick. Their look and their gameplay reach are unchanged.
- **Solid decor:** a decor piece can block walking (B, or the "Blocks walking" box under the selection line). New buildings, walls, big clutter and trees are placed solid; openings (gates, doorways, arches), bridges, flat pieces, cloth, the weapon rack and small clutter are not. Solid pieces over 45 units tall stop shots too. Every piece placed before the palette existed stays visual only.
- **Town in git:** `GET /api/town` serves the live town; `pnpm town:pull` writes it to `apps/server/data/town-layout.json`, which is bundled as the starting town. The daily `town-sync` workflow pushes a `town/live` branch when the live town changed (Actions cannot open PRs in this repo).

### The town editor palette

- **What it lists** (`game/townPalette.ts`): the 13 layout prop kinds first ("Town pieces", with what they do: the chest is the stash, the stall the trader), then every decor asset the server accepts, grouped as Buildings, Walls and fences, Props, Lights and fires, Graveyard, Dungeon and Nature, by name. Search matches every word against the name, group, hint ("the forge", "lit at night", "blocks") and asset id; groups start closed except the town pieces, and a search opens every group with a match.
- **Thumbnails** are rendered from the real models by the item icon renderer (`ui/itemIconRenderer.ts`, `requestObjectIcon`), seen from above at the game camera's angle, and cached for the session. A tile asks for its thumbnail only when it scrolls into view, so opening the editor loads the models of the tiles on screen, not the kit.
- **Placing:** pick a tile (the place tool, key 2), click the ground. The ghost outline is the piece's footprint. Q/E turn the next piece before it is placed.
- **Fires in town:** the camp fire and the brazier are decor (`campfire`, `brazier`) built in code in `props.ts`, lit and burning like the forge's fire, and like every lit decor piece they join the light budget and the world fires ([vfx.md](vfx.md#world-fires)).

### Scenery assets

- **Imported 2026-09-30** (`scripts/assets/import-kaykit.py`, the CC0 KayKit Medieval Hexagon, Dungeon Remastered and Halloween Bits packs from github.com/KayKit-Game-Assets, LICENSE files kept): 146 models, 6.3 MB in 293 files. Medieval: 26 buildings (red, and blue for homes, taverns, towers, markets, windmill and barracks), 7 wall and fence gates and corners, 10 props, 17 nature pieces. Dungeon: 15 walls, 5 iron barriers, 7 banners (brown and red: plain, shield, thin, triple), barrels, kegs, crates, trunks, bottles, tables, chairs, beds, 7 floor tiles, the lit thin candle. Halloween: gates, fences, graves, coffins, stepping stones, a shrine, a post, a decorated dead tree, and six candle pieces.
- **Left out, and why:** green and yellow buildings and flags (saturated team colours), the pattern banners (large white fields read bright at night), except the older checkered `dungeon_banner_blue`, which was registered before and stays placeable, tinted down (0x9c948c) so its white squares read as soiled cloth, pieces showing gold (`box_small_decorated`, the coin stacks) or pumpkins (`bench_decorated`, every pumpkin), autumn orange and yellow pines, clouds, the hex ground tiles and bare hex hills (yellow hex tops on free-form ground), wall-mounted pieces (mounted torch, shelves, sword displays, the hanging lantern), stairs that lead nowhere, grates and dug graves (pits that only work sunk into a floor), duplicate unlit dungeon candles (the Halloween ones are lit by the world fires instead), keys, plates and loose coins. Already registered but kept out of the palette (`town: false`): the chests (read as the stash), the coin pile and the pumpkin.
- **Sizes:** heights follow the older entries of the same kind (houses 130, towers 190 to 220, the castle 280, medieval clutter about 125 units per model unit, dungeon furniture about 15; about 30 units a metre). Flat tiles and stepping stones are sized by their thickness, so a large tile is about 105 across.
- **One texture per atlas:** every KayKit dungeon .glb embeds the same 16 KB atlas; `scripts/assets/share-dungeon-texture.mjs` rewrote the new ones as .gltf + .bin sharing one `dungeon_texture.png` (2.74 MB of .glb became 1.76 MB). The client (`shareTextures` in `render/assets.ts`) points every material at one texture per image file and sampler, so forty town pieces painted from the medieval atlas upload it once instead of forty times. The 19 older dungeon .glb files keep their own embedded copy.
- **Load on demand:** the registry is a list; a model file loads only when something built uses it (`PropBatch`) or a palette tile scrolls into view. A fresh load of the town fetched 57 model and texture files, none of them from the new kit; after saving a town with ten new pieces, exactly those ten models were added.
- **The shared list** (`packages/shared/src/world/townDecorAssets.ts`, written by `scripts/assets/town-decor.ts` from the client registry): every placeable asset with its footprint at scale 1 (measured from the file at its registered height, with the centre offset), height, collision shape (box, circle, or just the trunk for a tree), whether it is placed solid, and whether it is lit. The server validates saved towns against it and builds collision from it without loading any models. Run the script after changing `assets.ts`; `townPalette.test.ts` fails until the list matches.

## How

Code:

- Maps: `packages/shared/src/world/` (`town.ts` layout, stations, `validateLayout`; `worldPlan.ts` the world's tree of roads; `worldMap.ts` the world map, `townExits`, `placeTown`; `maps.ts` `wildsPlan`, `loadMap`, `buildMap` (uncached, whole), `freshWorld`, `waypointArrival`, `entranceArrival`, `placeName`; `zoneGen.ts` the plan's boss, entrances, camps and quotas, and the chunk generator `ZoneWorld`; `placement.ts` the placement index; `camps.ts` zone camps; `dungeon.ts` dungeons, the antechamber, the Arena gate and pit; `gamemap.ts` collision; `nav.ts` flow field; `gen.ts` rivers and obstacles; `types.ts` map descriptors). Region themes: `packages/shared/src/data/zones.ts`.
- Movement: `packages/shared/src/sim/movement.ts` (shared with client prediction).
- Dungeon exit: `Portal.sealed: 'boss'` (`world/types.ts`), `portalOpen`, `onBossKilled` and the loot shift in `sim/dungeon.ts`, the skip in `sim/players.ts`, `exitOpen` in `sim/snapshot.ts`; the server's refusal and chat line in `room.ts` (`tick`); the client keeps sealed portals out of `RoomView.def` until `exitOpen` (`game/game.ts` `openExits`) and draws the opened exit with `render/exitPortal.ts`.
- Server: `apps/server/src/manager.ts` (instances, rooms, portals, waypoints, `replaceTown`, idle closing), `room.ts`, `staging.ts` (ready check), `townStore.ts`.
- Client: `apps/client/src/game/townEditor.ts` (the editor), `townEditorPick.ts` (picking order, click cycling, layer rows and search, hide and lock bookkeeping), `townPalette.ts` (palette entries, groups and search), `apps/client/src/ui/TownEditorPanel.tsx`, `TownPalette.tsx`, `townThumbs.ts` and `townEditor.css`; the asset registry in `render/assets.ts`; rendering in `apps/client/src/render/props.ts` (town, gates, the Arena building, the wild border, `addUnderground` for dungeons, the world's light sources), built and drawn by chunk around the camera (`render/worldChunks.ts`, [world-streaming.md](world-streaming.md#step-2-client-rendering-by-chunk)); lighting in `scene.ts` (sun, moon, hero light), `lights.ts` (the light budget), `nightRim.ts` and `rigs/compile.ts` (enemy rim, albedo floor).
- Numbers: `WILDS`, `ZONE_SIZE`, `DUNGEON`, `NAV`, `GROUND` in `packages/shared/src/config/sim.ts`.

```ts
type MapDesc =
  | { kind: 'world'; seed; layout? } | { kind: 'staging'; seed; level } | { kind: 'dungeon'; seed; level; run }
  | { kind: 'arenaGate' } | { kind: 'arena' } | { kind: 'flat' } | { kind: 'testground' } | { kind: 'town'; layout? } | { kind: 'wilds'; seed };
```

`loadMap` is deterministic and cached (32 entries; the world's key includes the layout hash).

Town layout limits (`validateLayout`): at most 600 props, 80 paths of 200 points, 20 plazas, 20 portals, 800 decor of which at most 400 solid and 160 lit; size 800 to 6000; prop scale 0.3 to 3, decor scale 0.2 to 4. Every decor asset must be in `TOWN_DECOR_ASSETS`: a save naming an unknown one is rejected, while a town loaded from disk (`townStore.ts`, `unknownDecor: 'drop'`) drops just that piece, so an asset removed from the game later cannot replace the live town with the default one. Town saves are limited to one per 3 s per client.

Layout format: decor gained one optional field, `solid: true` (only `true` is stored; absent means visual only). A solid piece adds an obstacle of kind `decor` in `layoutToMap` (from `decorCollision`: 85% of the footprint box, a circle for round things, the trunk for trees), which the renderer skips because the model is drawn from the decor. Layouts without it load and serialise exactly as before: the live town's layout hash and built map are pinned in a test.

## Limits and open questions

- **Click-to-move from a blocked nav cell** (a hero pressed against a fence or in a tight yard of crates and trees, as by the pen west of the live town's square) starts its route from the nearest open cell in scan order, which can be the one across the fence; the hero then pushes into the fence until the click is changed. Minions pick the open cell nearest their actual position (`openCell` in `sim/minionPath.ts`); the client's `nearestWalkable` in `game/clickMove.ts` could do the same.
- **Pause is per room, not per spot:** a player alone far out in the world cannot pause either, since the town and the whole world share one room. Pausing by position would need `canPause` in the snapshot rather than the welcome.
- **Deleting the forge's rack** makes the game fall back to a rack beside the spawn.
- **Solid decor collides by its box:** an L-shaped wall corner or a building with a porch blocks its whole (inset) footprint, and walking through a gate model needs the piece left non-solid. Round and trunk shapes are chosen by asset id pattern in `town-decor.ts`.
- **Thumbnails are lit brighter than the game** (no night, no fog, no canvas grade), so a piece looks lighter in the palette than in town.
- **Mountains and hills with trees are hex-based tiles** from the medieval pack, meant for the outskirts where fog and distance hide the hex edge; up close in town they look like tiles.
- **Every lit decor piece gets a light source and a flame.** Many candles close together share the 8 real lights and fall back to ground pools like other sources; the limit of 160 lit pieces keeps the fire draw and the light list bounded but was not measured at that count.
- **The town waypoint is not in the layout,** so it is not a layer row: it follows the spawn point (it stands at the nearest open spot 170 east and 90 north of it).
- **Hide and lock are keyed by array position,** so they are cleared when the editor opens on a town that changed since they were set (another builder's save, or leaving without saving after a delete).
- **Station labels in the world** ("Forge", "Stash") stay where they were until the town is saved, since the editor preview rebuilds only the geometry.
- **The server does not stop deleting portals or the spawn;** only the editor does. A layout without a Wilds portal is rejected, but the world drops that portal from the town anyway.
- **An invalid town save is dropped without a notice** to the builder.
- **Dungeon exit:** it opens only at its fixed spot, not where the boss fell, because the map is generated on both sides from the descriptor and only a flag (`Snapshot.exitOpen`) travels. Loot is moved off it only when it opens; an item a player drops on the open exit later still needs a step into it to pick up. If a boss ever fails to spawn, the run can only be left through the antechamber.
- **Camps:** only the tent blocks; crates and barrels are walked through, like the rest of the generated decor. Tents and carts face the fire, but the models were not checked for which side is the front. A guard pack spreads 110 units around the fire, so monsters can stand in it (it does not hurt). The standalone `wildsMap` keeps its single camp at the spawn and gets no generated camps.
- **The standalone `wildsMap` and the `testground` map** are only used by tests and tools.
- **Lighting:** party lights were only checked in code, not with two players on screen. The town has only 4 lamp posts, so the square is lit mostly by the hero, the forge and the Arena torches; houses have no light of their own (windows or door lanterns would need a spot per building model). Ground pools draw over the ground only at a fixed height, so a pool under a torch on a wall does not climb the wall. The sandbox (`flat` theme) has no night. `?time=0.75` is already full night; dusk is about 0.55 to 0.65.
